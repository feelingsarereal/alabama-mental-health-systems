## What it is

For most of living memory, a mental health emergency in America has been handled by whoever answers 911. Police arrive. The person ends up in an emergency room or a cell, and neither was built for them. Federal guidance issued in 2025 describes what should exist in place of that, and reduces it to three things: someone to contact, someone to respond, and a safe place for help [110].

The first is a phone line. Since July 2022 that has meant 988, the national three-digit number for mental health, substance use and suicide crises [9]. The second is a mobile crisis team, which in the guidance's words delivers "rapid, on-site interventions to de-escalate crises and connect individuals to care" [110]. The third is a place that is neither a hospital ward nor a jail, a crisis center where a person can walk in or be brought and be seen straight away.

The guidance wants the three to work as one system serving "anyone, anywhere, at anytime" [110]. The reasoning is practical. A call line with no team to send and nowhere to refer can only talk. A crisis center that officers have never heard of stays quiet. Each part is worth far less alone.

## How it works in Alabama

Alabama built its crisis system almost from nothing after 2020, and it now has all three elements [13]. That is a real achievement. The harder questions are how far each element reaches and whether anyone can show what it accomplishes.

Start with the phone. Four call centers inside the state answer 988, and in FY2025 they took 40,053 calls [9] [16]. The figure the Department watches is the in-state answer rate, the share of calls picked up by an Alabama counselor before they roll over to a national backup. It rose from 82.5 percent in FY2024 to 87.4 percent in FY2025, though texts and chats still went to a national center [16]. The money is less settled than the service. The General Fund line for 988 call centers fell from $500,000 to zero in the FY2027 budget, and opioid-settlement appropriations have carried part of the cost [18] [91].

Mobile teams are the thinnest layer. The Department funds 14 teams at 11 community mental health centers, and five more for children and adolescents at three centers [13] [111]. In February 2025 mobile teams and crisis centers together covered 24 of the state's 67 counties, and the Department's plan was to grow to 23 teams [33]. Since October 2023 Medicaid has paid for a mobile response, defined as a two-person team available at any hour with follow-up for up to 72 hours [75].

The six crisis centers are in Mobile, Montgomery, Birmingham, Tuscaloosa, Huntsville and Dothan. They take walk-ins and police drop-offs around the clock [12], and since a 2022 law an officer may drive a person in crisis to a center and not to jail [225] [227]. The centers performed 9,929 evaluations in FY2025 [16]. Adult centers may not admit children, which leaves the 24-bed unit in Huntsville, for ages 12 to 18, as the only pediatric crisis unit on the Department's list [14] [41] [12].

Then came the argument. In September 2025 the state's evaluation commission reported that it "cannot verify" the emergency-room and jail diversions the Department had reported, because the centers counted them differently, and it put average utilization in FY2024 at 27 percent. The Department answered that "most centers function at capacity most days" [14]. The Legislature tied money to reporting. As reported on final passage, the FY2027 budget holds back the last two quarterly installments of the $42 million for crisis centers and rural crisis care until the Department reports on the programs [17].

## How this report uses it

Domain 3 takes the three elements in order and gives the figures. Domain 2 shows the budget lines. Domain 6 treats the evaluation dispute as a question about data: what counts as a "diversion", and who decides [14]? In Section 10 the same services come back as Intercept 0, the point before any arrest [4].

Two habits will help as you read. When the report says a crisis center "serves" a county, remember that the Department's list assigns 60 counties to a center and leaves seven out, and that being assigned says nothing about the length of the drive [12]. And when you meet a count of calls answered or evaluations performed, notice what kind of number it is. It tells you the door was open. Whether the person stayed out of a cell or an emergency room, and was seen by someone the following week, is the part Alabama cannot yet show.
