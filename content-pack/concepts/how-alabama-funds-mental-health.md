## What it is

Every spring the Alabama Legislature debates how much to give the Department of Mental Health, and a number is reported in the press. For FY2027 it was $240.9 million [18]. It is easy to take that for the state's mental health budget. It is about one-sixth of what the Department actually takes in.

The state passes two main budgets each year, one for the General Fund and one for the Education Trust Fund, and an agency can spend only what those acts appropriate to it [301]. The fiscal year runs from 1 October to 30 September, so "FY2027" begins in October 2026 [301]. An appropriation lasts one year. A program funded this session has no claim on the next.

Not all money comes that way, though. Some arrives by standing law: a tax is earmarked for a purpose and flows to it without an annual vote. Some is federal, either as the match on Medicaid claims or as grants. A block grant is a federal sum a state receives each year to spend on community mental health services, with a few obligations attached [302]. And some is one-time money, of which the opioid settlements are the current example.

## How it works in Alabama

The Department's operating revenue in FY2025 was $1.43 billion. State sources supplied 40.9 percent of it. The General Fund gave $239.8 million, or 16.8 percent, and the Special Mental Health Fund almost exactly as much, $236.4 million. The Education Trust Fund added $79.1 million. Everything else, 59.1 percent, was federal, local and miscellaneous money, and most of that was Medicaid [16].

Two things in those figures surprise people. The first is how much of the Department's budget is not about mental illness at all. Medicaid paid $568.0 million for community developmental-disability services, against $125.9 million for community mental-illness services [16]. The second is the Special Mental Health Fund, which rarely makes the news. State law sends part of a liquor tax into it and says the fund "shall be used only for mental health purposes" [303].

So why the attention to the General Fund? Because it is the money with choices in it. Medicaid follows the people who are enrolled and the services it covers. In the report's reading, the General Fund pays for what Medicaid does not: crisis centers, state hospital care for the uninsured, and services for adults without coverage [16]. In December 2024 the Department was considering a request for a General Fund increase of $68.6 million for FY2026 [21]. The enacted increase was about $4.7 million, and the FY2027 figure is lower again [20] [18].

The 2026 session added something new. A state evaluation commission had reported the previous September that it could not verify the crisis centers' results [14]. As reported on final passage, the FY2027 budget withholds the last two quarterly installments of the $42 million for crisis diversion and rural crisis-care centers until the Department reports the status of the programs to the budget chairs, the Finance Director and the Legislative Fiscal Officer [17]. An earlier Senate committee version had spelled out what to report: each center's operating expenses, employees and persons served [19]. The comparison sheets most people consult show amounts only. The condition sits in the text of the act, which could not be read for this report.

Settlement money has become a second channel. Act 2025-255 directed $27.9 million of opioid-settlement funds to the Department, including $3.5 million for 988 [91]. In 2026 a supplemental bill, HB487, appropriated a further $43.1 million across agencies, of which $26.4 million went to the Department [92]. Meanwhile the General Fund line for 988 call centers went from $500,000 in FY2026 to zero in FY2027 [18]. The weakness is that nothing settlement funds pay for recurs without a base appropriation.

Federal block grants are small by comparison, at $33.8 million in FY2025 [16], but they carry weight. Accepting the grant obliges the state to keep a planning council that includes people who use services [73] and to file the federal tables on which most national comparisons of Alabama rest [42].

Domain 2 lays out the revenue, the budget lines for FY2026 and FY2027, and the spending benchmarks. Read its table of General Fund lines beside the paragraph on settlement funds, and ask of each service which of the two is paying for it [20] [18] [91].
