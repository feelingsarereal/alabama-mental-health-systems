## What it is

Most people assume a clinic is paid for what it does, one item at a time. A therapy session has a price, a medication visit has another, and the clinic sends a bill for each. That is fee-for-service, and it is how Alabama Medicaid has paid community mental health centers: a set amount per procedure code, from the Agency's published schedule [49].

The trouble lies in what has no code. Nobody bills for ringing a client who missed an appointment, or for the hour spent finding a bed, or for keeping a crisis worker awake at three in the morning in case the phone rings. Work that earns nothing tends not to get done. So a system paid by the item ends up with plenty of items and very little joining them together. Alabama's own CCBHC manual makes the point from the other side when it lists care coordination as an activity that triggers no payment by itself [285].

The Certified Community Behavioral Health Clinic, or CCBHC, is a federal answer to that. It has two halves. The first is a list of obligations. A CCBHC must provide nine kinds of service, among them outpatient treatment for mental illness and substance use, round-the-clock crisis response, primary-care screening, case management and peer support [10], and it must serve anyone who comes, "regardless of their place of residence, ability to pay, or age" [287].

The second half is the payment. A clinic adds up what it costs in a year to run everything on the list, coordination and crisis cover included, and files that as a cost report. Dividing the total by the clinic's daily visits gives a rate for that clinic alone [285]. The rate is "prospective" because it is fixed ahead of time. Alabama uses the daily version, known as PPS-1, under which the clinic receives "a single payment each day when at least one CCBHC triggering event service is provided to a Medicaid enrollee" [285]. One visit or four on the same day, the payment is the same.

## How it works in Alabama

The federal CCBHC Medicaid demonstration began in 2017 [74], and on 4 June 2024 Alabama was named one of ten states added to it [25]. Only a community mental health center designated by the Department may apply, which keeps the model inside the existing network of 19 centers [10] [31]. A clinic may contract some of the nine services out to partner organizations, though it must deliver at least 51 percent of encounters itself [10].

Conversion is happening a few centers at a time. WellStone in Huntsville and AltaPointe in Mobile were certified from July 2024 [26]. Mountain Lakes received federal approval in October 2025 and Highland Health Systems in April 2026 [27]. The Department's own directory lists a fifth clinic that its May 2026 announcement does not name [28], and six more providers received grants in August 2026 to prepare [29]. The Department describes its aim as a statewide transformation [287].

The details of what triggers a payment say a good deal about the model's intent. A visit with a certified peer specialist is enough to trigger the daily rate. Case management does not trigger it but is carried as a cost inside it [288]. Fee schedules under-pay both.

Early evidence is thin and encouraging. The first two clinics served 30,275 people in FY2025 [16]. At WellStone the wait for an initial evaluation fell from 18 days in January 2025 to less than one day in December 2025, by the Department's account [30]. No clinical outcome measures, such as hospital or emergency-room use, were found in the Department's published reports as of October 2026.

There is one firm limit. The daily rate is paid for Medicaid enrollees [105]. A CCBHC must still see the uninsured, and in a state that has not expanded Medicaid there are many of them, but the rate does not pay for their care.

## How this report uses it

Section 12 sets out the model and names the clinics. Read it beside Domain 2, where the crisis services are shown living on General Fund lines that the Legislature can cut or attach conditions to [17] [18]. The report's argument is that a CCBHC moves some of that work, for people with Medicaid, inside a payment the clinic earns in the ordinary way. Domain 3 makes the companion point about hand-offs: when one clinic is paid for crisis response, outpatient care and follow-up together, the gaps between them become that clinic's problem [10] [105].

Keep two cautions in mind as you read. The phrase "all 19 centers will convert" appears in no Department source, and the report avoids it [27]. And a center that has received a preparation grant is not yet a CCBHC.
